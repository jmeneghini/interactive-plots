An ideal gas under a free piston

Hard-disk molecules bounce around a box closed by a heavy piston that slides without friction. Gravity pulls only on the piston, so the gas holds it up by pressure alone. Every collision is elastic. Molecules are coloured by speed.

Units: molecule mass m = 1, Boltzmann's constant k = 1, box width W = 2, ceiling at height 2. Pressure in 2D is a force per length, and the ideal gas law reads PA = NkT with A the area.

The piston settles where the gas pressure balances its weight, P = Mg/W. Without the heat bath the total energy E is shared between the gas (NkT), the piston's height energy and the piston's own thermal motion, which predicts the final temperature kT = (E − 2rMg)/(2N + 3/2) and height y = (N + 1)kT/(Mg) + 2r.

Doubling M at once compresses the gas irreversibly: the piston overshoots, rings and then settles, and the gas ends hotter than when M is doubled slowly (over 40 time units). Turn collisions off (with the heat bath off) to see that a perfectly ideal gas in a box with smooth walls never thermalizes: only the piston can change vy, and nothing changes vₓ.

With the heat bath on, the floor is a thermal wall. The gas relaxes to kTbath, even with collisions off, and the piston settles at y = (N + 1)kTbath/(Mg) + 2r. Work done compressing the gas flows out to the bath as heat (Q < 0).
