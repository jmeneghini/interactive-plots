Neutral mesons as vectors in flavor space

A neutral, flavor-diagonal quark–antiquark state is a combination a uū + b dd̄ + c ss̄, so it is a vector (a, b, c) in a three-dimensional space whose axes are uū, dd̄ and ss̄. Drag to rotate the figures; scroll to zoom.

1. π⁰, η₈ and η₁ form a rotated orthonormal frame

π⁰ = (uū − dd̄)/√2,   η₈ = (uū + dd̄ − 2ss̄)/√6,   η₁ = (uū + dd̄ + ss̄)/√3

The three states are unit vectors that are mutually orthogonal, so going from the quark basis to the SU(3) basis is a single rigid rotation. The slider carries the uū, dd̄, ss̄ axes onto π⁰, η₈, η₁. η₁ lies along the body diagonal, the SU(3)-symmetric direction. π⁰ and η₈ span the plane perpendicular to it, where a + b + c = 0 (the octet plane). Looking straight down η₁ turns the three quark axes into the quark-triplet weight triangle.

2. Mixing: the physical states are rotated in the η₈–η₁ plane

SU(3) breaking (ms ≠ mu,d) mixes η₈ and η₁. π⁰ stays put, and the physical pair is η₈, η₁ rotated by an angle θ about the π⁰ axis:

The vector nonet works the same way: replace η₈, η₁ by ω₈, ω₁. At ideal mixing, tan θ = 1/√2 (θ ≈ 35.3°), the rotated frame lands on the quark axes: one state is pure ss̄ (the φ) and the other is (uū + dd̄)/√2 (the ω). The pseudoscalars are far from that because the U(1)A anomaly gives η′ extra mass.

The θ values are the PDG quark-model estimates, quadratic mass formula for θP and linear for θV. Sign and phase conventions vary between references: an overall sign on a state (φ ↔ −φ) does not change it physically.

3. Flavor quantum numbers live in the same space

A diagonal flavor charge assigns a number to each quark: q = (qu, qd, qs). Read the same way, it is a vector in this space. Isospin I₃ points along π⁰, hypercharge Y along η₈ and baryon number B along η₁. Charge relations then become vector addition. Q has no η₁ component, so Tr Q = 0. The squared charges Q² do have one, and the projections of Q² give the relative two-photon couplings of π⁰, η₈ and η₁, which come out as 1 : 1/√3 : 2√2/√3.
